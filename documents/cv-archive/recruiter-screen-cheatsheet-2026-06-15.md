# Recruiter-Screen Cheat Sheet — Joel Goldfoot
*For the first call with an executive recruiter (AboveBoard, Riviera, or any inbound). ~20-30 min, calibration, and mutual. Read it 10 minutes before the call. Built 2026-06-15. Tone: confident, specific, no hedging.*

---

## The six questions they'll ask, and your answers

**1. "Walk me through your background / tell me your story."**
> I define how AI systems behave with the people and agents they serve. When an agent does the work, the experience is no longer the screen, it is the behavior: how the system expresses confidence, when it hands off to a human, how it recovers when it is wrong. That behavioral layer is the new UX, and it is the layer I own. I lead a global UX, research, and content org at VP scope, I have shipped two production behavioral contracts that govern exactly that, and I wrote the book on it: *How to Lead Design in the AI Era*.

**2. "What are you looking for?"**
> A role where defining how AI products behave is the core problem, not a side project, with VP-level scope and authority. That shows up two ways: VP or Head of Product Design at an AI-native company, or a VP/Head of AI Product or AI Experience that owns the behavioral layer directly. The mandate and the scope matter more to me than the exact title.

**3. "What are your comp expectations?"** *(state it directly, do not dance)*
> I'm targeting $350K base. I'm flexible on how the total gets there, equity matters and I'll weigh it seriously, but base is the anchor. At an AI-native company I'm looking at $500K and up total.

**4. "Why are you looking? Why leave Conviva?"** *(lead with what you built; never a word against Conviva)*
> I spent the last two years building the operating model everyone is now trying to hire for: two production behavioral contracts, and an AI-native pipeline where design and product co-author specs that ship through code generation. It is proven and published. What I can't do there is scale it, I'm one of a few builders in the company and the only one on the design side. I'm not leaving a bad situation. I'm leaving a proven one to run it at the scale it deserves.

**5. "Where are you based, and what's your timeline?"** *(project selectivity, not urgency)*
> San Francisco. Open to SF or US-remote, no relocation. I'm employed and looking selectively, so I'm moving deliberately for the right mandate rather than rushing. I'd rather find the right seat than a fast one.

**6. "Can you share a portfolio?"** *(the trap — pivot to systems and outcomes)*
> My work isn't a portfolio of screens, it's an operating model and a methodology. The strongest artifacts are the behavioral contracts, the spec-to-ship pipeline, the open framework, and the outcomes: an $11M ARR product, a three-week prototype that closed a $1M annual contract, the Gartner Visionary placement. I can walk through any of it. If a process is built purely around a visual portfolio review, flag it early, because my edge is judgment and systems, not pixels.

## Questions YOU ask them (this call is mutual, you're screening the recruiter too)
- What searches do you have right now that fit a VP-scope design or AI-experience leader, SF or remote, at $350K-plus base?
- Are your active searches design-titled or product/AI-titled? I'm open to both if the mandate is right.
- Where are you seeing companies put ownership of AI product behavior, under design, product, or engineering?
- How do you typically work with a candidate, and what's your timeline?

## Comp quick-reference (for when numbers come up)
- **Target:** $350K base. **Floor:** $300K base. **Total:** $500K-plus at AI-native companies. **Walk-away:** below $300K base (rare exception only for top-tier-lab transformative equity).
- Variable and bonus do **not** substitute for base. Base is the anchor.

## Discretion guards (you're still employed)
You CAN be candid with a recruiter in confidence. But do NOT name:
- Any customer (e.g., the Fortune 500 retail account).
- The Salesforce / Agentforce work, at all.
- Anything under NDA (TensorMesh).

Do NOT volunteer Conviva's financial situation, layoffs, or a hard deadline. Project selectivity. Urgency weakens leverage.
